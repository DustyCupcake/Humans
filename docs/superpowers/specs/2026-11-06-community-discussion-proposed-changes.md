# Helping out in Humans: proposed changes for discussion

**Status: draft for community comment. Nothing here is decided or built.** Each change below says what it is, what it would mean for you, and what we'd like to hear. Items marked **[OPEN]** are undecided. Who coordinates and how people are chosen and renewed is a separate conversation in the org structure group, so this document doesn't settle it.

The goal: anyone can see what needs doing, how big it is and whether it fits them, and step in. Every team and working group always has an invitation out.

## 1. Roles, tasks and shifts

**What it is.** Three kinds of work:
- A **role** is a standing responsibility, like coordinator or contact point. It ends when it is handed over.
- A **task** is a piece of work with an end, like writing the power request form. It ends when it is done.
- A **shift** is being somewhere for a time slot. Shifts work as they do now.

**What changes for you.** Teams can list tasks as well as roles. Nothing is removed.

**We'd like to hear:** does this split match how your team thinks about its work?

## 2. Roles carry on from year to year

**What it is.** Roles stay small: a title, who holds it, and a short description. They aren't copied each year; they continue until handed over. A role can have more than one holder, and holders can see each other, so they can talk if someone goes quiet.

**[OPEN]** Whether holders confirm each year that they still hold it. This is part of the org structure discussion.

## 3. What a task is

**What it is.** A task has a title, a short description, how many people it needs, a rough size in hours, how critical it is, and a deadline or a window of dates. It can have sub-tasks (three levels deep), a checklist, and "waits for" links to other tasks. A task belongs to a person or to its team or working group. It doesn't belong to a role.

**What changes for you.** A task someone else could help with is described well enough for a stranger to take it on.

**We'd like to hear:** is a short description enough, and what else would you need before taking a task?

## 4. Everything is open and visible

**What it is.** Every task and role is visible and can be claimed. The exception is sensitive ones, which are hidden.

**What changes for you.** You can see what any team is doing, and what's unfilled, without being a member. Writing descriptions becomes a coordinator's first job. Roles that today have only a title carry over and are flagged until someone describes them.

**[OPEN]** Whether "sensitive" is set per task or only per team or working group.

## 5. Get involved

**What it is.** One page across the organisation listing invitations: tasks and roles with an unfilled place, with the team shown. Understaffed and critical ones come first.

**What changes for you.** It replaces the roster as the place to find where you can help.

**We'd like to hear:** what would you filter by (size, team, when, skills)?

## 6. How people join in

**What it is.** Small tasks you take straight away. Larger or critical ones need a coordinator to confirm, unless you've already finished a task in that team or working group. A requirement such as a signed confidentiality agreement is never waived because something is urgent. Later, a track record may also inform confirmation. It would never mean automatic rejection, and it would weigh the size of the work.

**What stays the same.** Joining a team and membership work as they do now. Holding a role still makes you a member. What a smaller task does to membership and Drive or email access is still being decided: for now, access follows membership, and we may separate it later.

**[OPEN]** What "Take part" means: following a team's activity, or the existing join.

**We'd like to hear:** is "finished something here before" a fair way to skip confirmation?

## 7. Support for coordinators

**What it is.** A coordinator's page shows what needs attention: tasks with unfilled places, overdue or blocked tasks, critical gaps, and requests waiting for an answer. It is generated from the tasks. Nobody edits it, so work can't hide in a private list. Roles with several holders share the same view, so splitting the work is a conversation between them.

**What changes for you.** Fewer lists to maintain. Everything that needs attention is visible to the community too.

## 8. Production view and projects

**What it is.** A view of all tasks across teams and working groups, as a list and a timeline, including what waits on what. Production can see where the event stands without asking each team. It also shows **projects**: a named group of related tasks, each living in its own team, with a shared goal, dates, a lead, and progress computed from its tasks. A project has no places of its own to fill.

**[OPEN]** How Production adds tasks to other teams' lists: directly with a permission, or by proposing tasks the coordinator adopts. Proposals would also let anyone suggest a task for a team.

## 9. Named permissions

**What it is.** Today the coordinator role decides most of what a person can do in a team. The proposal splits that into named permissions (manage shifts, edit the budget, manage members), granted by a role. Coordinators keep exactly what they have on the day it ships.

**What changes for you.** At first, nothing. Afterwards a team can create a role with only the powers it needs. Later, a task could carry a permission while someone holds it. That needs separate approval. The long-term aim is for named permissions to replace most of the separate admin roles, one at a time, each with its own approval.

## 10. Meetings in one place

**What it is.** One place for teams and working groups to hold meetings, with agenda, a scheduling poll, attendance and notes. Meetings show on the calendar. Working groups still see and use their own meetings.

**We'd like to hear:** what do you do today to run a meeting, and what's missing?

## 11. Notes, handovers and updates

**What it is.** Roles and tasks have a notes area that any holder can edit, so how things are done doesn't live in one head. When someone releases a role, they're prompted to leave a handover note, which the next holder sees first. A team's page shows its contact point and its latest update, posted by the team. Milestones appear as dated markers on tasks and projects.

**What changes for you.** You can see who to ask and what a team has been up to without asking, and a new holder starts with the last holder's notes.

**[OPEN]** Who posts the team's update: a reporter role, the coordinator, or anyone on the team.

**We'd like to hear:** where handovers fail today, and what a team would want to tell the community regularly.

## 12. The yearly cycle

**What it is.** Tasks are copied for the next event year. Last year's tasks, finished or not, drop out of the current lists and stay in history, where one that was never finished is still shown as unfinished. Roles aren't copied.

## 13. Finding things: menus and search

**What it is.** The top bar becomes three menus (Get involved, The event, Association), plus Admin for those who have access. A search box (Cmd/Ctrl-K) lists any page you can open. On phones, a bottom tab bar replaces the crowded top bar. Admin pages move out of member menus.

**What changes for you.** Less sideways scrolling, and pages you can reach by typing. This can ship on its own, ahead of the rest.

**[OPEN]** The names of the menus and what goes under each, and whether pinned pages are stored in your browser or on your account.

## Other proposals that need more input before anything is built

These came up in the earlier drafts and are not in the rollout above. Each needs more input from the community, or evidence from tasks in use, before it is worth building. Under each: what it is, why it waits, and what we need to hear.

### Helping people find and join

**Following, comments and questions.** Follow a team to hear what's happening, comment on a task, ask a team a question and see whether it has been answered. *Waits because* it adds a lot to learn and to maintain, and we don't yet know which parts teams would use. *We need:* which of these teams ask for once they're using tasks.

**Proposing a task.** Anyone suggests a task for a team, and the coordinator adopts it. *Waits because* we haven't decided whether Production gets this or a direct permission (see the open item under the production view). *We need:* a decision on that, and whether anyone, or only members, can propose.

**Task requirements.** A task can require something before confirmation, such as a qualification (electrician, medic) or a signed confidentiality agreement. *Waits because* the checking has to work for several kinds of requirement. *We need:* the list of requirements teams really use, and who checks each.

**Separating access from membership.** Email lists and shared drives follow team membership today. They could instead be an opt-in, or part of a small role such as "shared drive access", so not everyone gets a team's mail. *Waits because* it changes how existing access works. *We need:* how teams use their lists and drives, and who should get what.

### Knowing how it's going

**Reporting layers.** Two layers on a team's timeline: what production asks of a team, and what a team flags itself as a key item, with a reporter who gives the regular update. (Milestones as dated markers are proposed above.) *Waits because* it depends on tasks being in real use, and on the reporter question in the org doc. *We need:* what teams want to report, and how often.

**Handbook pages.** Longer "how we do things here" pages for a team, with edit history, that any holder can edit. (Notes and the handover prompt are proposed above.) *Waits because* it's a bigger feature and adds writing for volunteers. *We need:* what teams would put in a handbook, and where that lives today.

**Year-end review.** Each team reviews its year before the All Leads Meeting. *Waits because* it's a procedure first and software second. *We need:* what the review should cover.

**The production calendar and generated tasks.** Phases of the event year and dated asks to teams, such as "define placement and power needs by 1 May", that create tasks in each team automatically. *Waits because* Production will first add tasks by hand, and it's undecided where the calendar belongs. *We need:* whether teams get the same deadlines every year, which is the signal that automation would pay off.

**Requests.** One pattern for when a team needs answers from others: define, sign off, send, answer by the deadline, review, with last year's answers prefilled. *Waits because* it's the least worked-out idea. *We need:* which yearly requests exist today and how they're handled.

### Choosing and renewing people

These are part of the org structure discussion and are not decided here.

**Endorsement and candidacy.** Coordination slots advertised openly, with candidates endorsed by people who know them, and a published appointment if a slot stays empty. *Waits because* it's a governance decision. *We need:* who can endorse, how many endorsements, and who can appoint.

**Yearly re-affirmation.** Holders confirm each year that they still hold a standing role. *Waits because* it's a governance decision. A lighter alternative is to flag a role whose active holders fall below the number it needs, with no per-person check. *We need:* which fits the community.

**Escalation and gap cover.** Requests nobody answers move up to the parent team, then to recruitment. A department with no coordinator is covered by others. *Waits because* automatic moves change people's standing, and the thresholds are undecided. We'd start with reminders that a person acts on, not automatic changes. *We need:* what delay is reasonable, and who should be notified.

### Care and fairness

**Track record.** A person's finished tasks and shift attendance shown beside a request, as context and not a score, weighted by the size of the work. A "My contribution" page for the person themselves. *Waits because* attendance isn't recorded today, and we haven't agreed what counts. *We need:* what should be shown, for how long, and who sees it.

**Capacity and care.** A yearly "how I like to help" page: how many hours you can offer, task size you prefer, and reasons for declining. People near their limit are shown smaller tasks. Opt-in boards for barrios. *Waits because* it's personal data and nudges, and there's no workload problem in task data yet to measure against. *We need:* whether overload is a problem teams see, and what people are comfortable sharing.

**Concerns.** One way to raise a concern about a task, a team or a person in a role, with routes, a log, a decision, and deselection panels. *Waits because* it's a separate proposal with many open questions in the org doc: one pool or two for handling and facilitation, whether a consent decision can remove someone from a role, appeals, and who approves the settings. *We need:* answers to those from the org structure group and Participant Wellness.

### Making sure critical work gets done

**Paid roles as a backstop.** One option being discussed in the org structure group for two problems at once: covering critical work we can't find volunteers for, and defining what the paid work is. Paid roles would do the critical tasks nobody has picked up by their deadline, so the paid work is whatever the task list shows volunteers haven't covered, visible to everyone. A volunteer who takes such a task takes priority over the paid role. How many paid roles there would be, and how they're defined, is still open there. *Waits because* that is undecided and it depends on tasks being in real use. *We need:* what you'd want to see about paid work, and what counts as critical.

### On-site work and shifts

**Build and strike planning.** On-site tasks held by leads, with the number of crew needed per day suggesting a shift's minimum and maximum, a lead-by date, and a day sheet. *Waits because* on-site work is shifts, and a task must never record who turned up. *We need:* what leads use today to plan crews.

**Shifts improvements.** A prompt to confirm attendance, timing of when someone bailed, a notification and reply for no-shows, and a rest-day nudge. *Waits because* it changes what is recorded about people. It's also the data a track record would need. *We need:* what shift coordinators want recorded and who sees it.


## How it would roll out

1. **Now:** tasks for production planning, with dates, dependencies, assignees and claiming. Team coordinators confirm claims and manage their team's tasks, and production manages the plan. Tasks are visible to all members and show on team pages. A Get involved page lists tasks that still need people. Production can load a whole plan, with its dependencies, in one go, so a large existing plan doesn't have to be typed in by hand.
2. **In parallel:** named permissions (no visible change), meetings, and navigation.
3. **Then:** open roles on Get involved beside tasks, the coordinator view, and working groups.
4. **Then:** sub-tasks and projects, notes and handovers, milestones, and team updates.
5. **Later:** tasks that carry permissions, after separate sign-off.

None of this depends on the org structure decisions.

## How to comment

Reply on the community thread. For each change it helps to say what would work for your team, what wouldn't, and what is missing.
